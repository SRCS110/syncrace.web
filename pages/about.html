The fold

How to make the curve

Take a long strip of paper. Fold it in half, always in the same direction. Fold it again, and again, ten times in all. Now open it up so that every crease sits at exactly 90°.

What you're holding is a dragon curve: one line of 1,024 straight steps joined by 1,023 right-angle turns. However many times you fold, the line never runs back over itself. Corners can touch, but no step is ever drawn twice.

The rule

Simple steps, whole result

Every new fold follows one rule: keep the line you have, add a right turn, then add the same line again, backwards and mirrored.

One foldR
Two foldsR · R · L
Three foldsR R L · R · R L L
Ten folds1,023 creases, each one decided by the folds before it.

No single fold is complicated. The order is what makes the whole thing hold together.

Like Sync Race

We take all the folds and unwind them into one chain.

A local business has a lot of folds. A website. A Google listing. Ads. Business cards and flyers. The spreadsheet that runs the bookings. Usually each one was made by someone different, at a different time, and they pile up on top of each other: different hours here, an old logo there, a flyer pointing at a page that no longer exists.

We open all of them out into a single line.

  • No overlap. One partner, so no two vendors are doing the same job, or undoing each other's.
  • One direction. Every piece hands off to the next: a flyer's QR code to the website, the website to your Google profile, the profile to booking.
  • Nothing out of sync. Same name, hours, look and message everywhere a customer might find you.
  • Each step on its own. You can start with one fold and add the rest later. The line still runs true.
