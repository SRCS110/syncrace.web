Backlinks: why they matter and how a local business can earn them

November 21, 2026 · 6 min read · Sync Race Studios

A backlink is a link to your website from someone else’s. To search engines, each good one is a vote of confidence, and for a small business a handful of the right ones can matter more than anything else you change on your site.

Why backlinks matter

Search engines use links to discover pages and to judge which ones to trust. A link from a respected, relevant site tells Google and Bing that your business is real and worth showing. AI assistants lean on the same signals: the businesses they recommend tend to be the ones other reputable sites already mention and link to.

Links also send visitors directly. A link from your local chamber of commerce or a neighbourhood guide brings people who are already looking for what you do.

Quality beats quantity

One link from a local newspaper, a trade association or a well-known business in your area is worth more than hundreds from directories nobody visits. The links that count are:

  • Relevant: from sites connected to your industry or your area.
  • Trusted: from real organisations with real readers.
  • Earned: given because your business, page or work deserved it.

Where a local business can earn links

  • Your Google Business Profile and key directories. Start with Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp and the directories for your industry. Keep your name, address and phone number identical everywhere.
  • Local organisations. Chambers of commerce, business associations and downtown groups often list members with a link.
  • Suppliers, partners and clients. Businesses you work with may have a "partners", "stockists" or "where to find us" page. Ask to be included.
  • Sponsorships and community events. Sponsoring a school fundraiser, sports team or festival usually earns a mention and a link on their site.
  • Local press. A new location, an anniversary, a community project or an unusual story can earn coverage from local news sites and blogs.
  • Useful content. A genuinely helpful guide, checklist or local resource on your site gives others a reason to link to you without being asked.
  • Expert quotes. Offer your expertise to local journalists and industry blogs; quoted sources are often linked.

What to avoid

Buying links, swapping links in bulk, or using services that promise hundreds of backlinks breaks Google’s spam policies. At best those links are ignored; at worst they can hurt how your site ranks. The same goes for stuffing your link into comment sections and low-quality directories.

If an offer sounds fast and cheap, it’s the wrong kind of link.

Keep track of what you earn
