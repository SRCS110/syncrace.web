Before you spend on Google Ads, set up conversion tracking

November 21, 2026 · 5 min read · Sync Race Studios

Many small businesses try Google Ads, see clicks but no obvious results, and give up. The most common reason isn’t the ads. It’s that nothing was measuring what happened after the click.

Clicks are not customers

Google Ads will happily report clicks and impressions. What you actually care about is calls, form enquiries, bookings and sales. Conversion tracking connects the two, so you can see which keywords and ads produced real customers and which only spent money.

What to track

  • Phone calls from your ads and from your website.
  • Form submissions, such as contact, quote or booking requests.
  • Bookings or purchases completed on your site.
  • Directions requests, if foot traffic matters to you.

Track a few things that clearly mean business, not every click on the page. Google Analytics 4 and Google Ads can share these conversions, and a consent banner keeps the tracking respectful of visitors' privacy choices.

Why tracking makes the ads themselves better

Google's automated bidding learns from your conversions. With good tracking, it spends more on the searches that bring in customers and less on the ones that don't. Without it, the system optimises for clicks, which is exactly the wrong goal for most local businesses.

Other basics to get right first

  • Send clicks to the right page. Someone searching for "emergency plumber" should land on your emergency service page, not your home page.
  • Use negative keywords so you don't pay for searches like "plumber jobs" or "free".
  • Set a clear budget based on what a new customer is worth to you, and treat the first month as a test.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile complete, so local ads can show your location, hours and reviews.
