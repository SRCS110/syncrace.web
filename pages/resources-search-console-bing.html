Why your website needs Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools

November 21, 2026 · 5 min read · Sync Race Studios

Launching a website doesn’t mean search engines know it exists. Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools are free, take minutes to set up, and are the only way to see your site the way search engines do.

What these tools actually do

Both tools work the same way. You prove you own the website, then they show you:

  • Which pages are indexed, meaning eligible to appear in search, and which aren't, with the reason.
  • Which searches you appear for, how often people see your listing, and how often they click.
  • Problems, such as pages that won't load, mobile issues, broken structured data or security warnings.

Without them you're guessing. With them, you can see that a new page hasn't been indexed yet, or that people are finding you for a service you barely mention, and act on it.

Submit your sitemap

A sitemap is a file that lists every page on your site. Submitting it in both tools tells Google and Bing exactly what to crawl, instead of waiting for them to discover pages through links. For a new site, or a page you've just added, that can be the difference between showing up in days and showing up in weeks.

Why Bing matters more than it used to

Bing's own share of searches is smaller than Google's, but its index reaches further than its search box. It powers Microsoft Copilot and feeds other search engines, and it has been widely reported as one of the sources behind ChatGPT's web search. As more people ask AI assistants for recommendations, being properly indexed in Bing is part of being visible.

Bing Webmaster Tools also supports IndexNow, a way for your site to tell participating search engines the moment a page changes, and you can import a site you've already verified in Google Search Console in a few clicks.

Check in once a month

You don't need to live in these dashboards. Once a month is enough to:

  • Look for new indexing errors and fix them.
  • See which searches are growing, and write more about those topics.
  • Confirm new pages have been picked up.
